# Editions and discovery

Status: proposed product behavior.

## Shared daily edition

Provide a common editorial baseline: major developments, concise context, uncertainty, source links, and an “outside your radar” segment. Each segment answers: what is new, what led here, why it matters, and what remains uncertain.

## Personalized editions

Build from the same eligible claims and topic timelines. Add followed topics, adjust depth, and explain developments since the reader's last edition. Preserve room for useful unfamiliar subjects. Personalization changes selection and explanation while keeping factual grounding consistent.

## Podcast

Create the written briefing and podcast script from a common versioned edition. Keep a source-linked transcript and corrections trail. Audio generation and hosting are future implementation choices; no podcast exists yet.

## Discovery policy to evaluate

Balance public importance, timeliness, novelty, topic/source diversity, followed interests, and deliberate exploration. Explain why an unfamiliar topic was included. Do not equate engagement or semantic similarity with importance. The weights and evaluation method are open decisions.

## Evaluation questions

Can readers explain what changed? Do they encounter useful unfamiliar topics? Are claims supported and uncertainty visible? Can they navigate from a daily update to the topic's history? Compare editions against an agreed editorial rubric before automating publication.
